# 3D loading, performance and low-end PC audit

Date:2026-11-14. Baseline: `main` at `c7b88d752ce7594674ee0e6c38bf636732cb6195`, package 0.1.0, installed Three.js 0.187.0. Task 003 was added to the ongoing Task 002 investigation; its specification decisions and 129-row review are in `IMPLEMENTATION_READINESS.md`. Only audit documentation was created. Source, tests, seven original GLBs, GAMEPLAY, Git history and stashes were not changed. Neither excluded design-reference directory was inspected. No deployment or optimization was performed.

**Finding:** there are substantial asset, initialization and rendering costs. All seven GLBs total 74,418,232 bytes (70.97 MiB). The six characters are sequentially loaded and rigged; every consumer reparses downloaded bytes. Rover materials/primitives produce hundreds of draw submissions. The default terrain alone has 165,888 triangles, with dynamic shadows, environment-map preparation and a full procedural fallback rover. On this software renderer the crew room averaged 4.26 FPS and Mars 1.59–1.65 FPS. These diagnose a heavy workload; they do **not** establish FPS on an integrated or discrete GPU. No performance improvement is claimed.

### Reprocessing and network timeout

- After seven loads, cached Kavi repeated with **no fetch** but 110.4 ms parse and 385.4 ms rig preparation, plus a new renderer/first-frame cost. Aaryan, evicted from the 3-entry byte cache, fetched again 43.7 ms, parsed 110.0 ms and rigged 426.4 ms. These single warm repetitions vary with GC/scheduling; they demonstrate repeated work, not a reliable warm-load regression percentage.
- `assets.ts:11–24` caches `Promise<ArrayBuffer>` by URL. Hits do not refresh insertion order, so this is FIFO-like eviction, not LRU. The 3-entry bound cannot retain all six crew plus rover; pending entries can also be evicted. The largest three original assets alone could retain~38.06 MiB of downloaded bytes, separately from parsed/decoded assets. The cache has no byte budget, progress or cancellation ownership.
- `AbortSignal.timeout(30_000)` applies to fetch/body completion, not parse/rig/upload. A successful download followed by slow preparation may therefore appear stuck without timing out. Rejected cached entries are removed; the loader does not automatically retry. CharacterPreview has a manual Retry control; the room/Mars use fallbacks without equivalent per-model retry controls.
- **Measured timeout:** load Lautaro at 4 Mbps, 80 ms latency, no HTTP cache → `AbortError: The user aborted a request.` at 30,000.4 ms. Its 16.80 MB body needs at least 33.60 seconds at that throughput before latency/processing, so the total 30-second deadline is inherently too short for this legitimate connection. Turning off throttling and repeating the same loader call succeeded in 255.2 ms; a rejected promise did not poison the cache.
- This identifies two independent failure modes: large downloads exceed the deadline; cached downloads still incur parsing, runtime rig work and new-context uploads. Increasing the timeout alone cannot solve the latter.

Late arriving models are disposed if a consumer has unmounted. Downloads generally continue after unmount; there is no shared-consumer cancellation contract.
- Room cleanup removes DOM buttons/listeners, cancels animation and disposes grid/scene/renderer. Isolated component switching showed old renderer disposal and one attached canvas, with no idle redraws. Production scene records showed one live attached renderer at a time; no evidence of simultaneous room/preview/Mars loops. A browser may retain a disposed context until GC; “one active renderer” is not proof of one driver context allocation.
- Mars traverses geometry/materials and disposes imported rover/environment target; it does not explicitly walk every procedural texture. **Important version-specific correction:** installed `node_modules/three/src/renderers/WebGLRenderer.js:1088–1116` calls `textures.dispose()`, and `webgl/WebGLTextures.js:391–413` destroys tracked texture/render-target resources. Observed counts reached 0/0 after renderer disposal, including the initial Mars renderer. Therefore omission of per-texture calls does **not** prove a GPU texture leak in this version. Explicit ownership remains useful for future renderer reuse and CPU ImageBitmap lifetime, and a long driver-memory soak is still required.
- Shader/program count was 20 in the loaded room and 22 on default Mars. The studio's first readback wait was 568–839 ms versus 35–53 ms on a warm draw. New renderers repeatedly incur initialization; extending caching must not retain all those GPU resources without a memory budget.
- Development StrictMode can mount/clean/remount effects. The principal scene FPS/transition results above use the production build; do not confuse development initialization duplication with two simultaneously active game renderers.

## 5. Compatibility and correctness hazards before optimization

1. **Old/integrated GPUs:** Three 0.187 requestsWebGL2; the current Mars/launch constructors do not catch context-creation failure the way CrewRoom/CharacterPreview do. A driver-disabled or unsupported context can break the world. No actual weak integrated GPU was available; there is no supported-device floor yet.
2. **Memory/bandwidth:** roughly 648 MiB potential RGBA image storage across the seven files, plus decoded images, byte cache, geometry, shaders, buffers/shadows and browser overhead. Available system RAM is not a safe substitute for GPU/shared-memory headroom. DPR 2 costs four times the pixels of DPR 1; a strong CPU does not imply this is affordable.
3. **Quality can currently hide collision scenery:** v2 quality handling (`MarsWorld.tsx:683`) lowers rendered instanced-rock counts without filtering the already-created `obstaclePositions`. A removed decorative instance that participates in collision can become an invisible obstacle. v1 versusv 2 also changes seeded rock distribution/radii/collision inclusion (`:226–265`); **visual variant must never be used as an automatic quality switch**.
4. **Quality restoration is not symmetric:** v2 pebbles set `count=Math.min(target,currentCount)`. Once reduced to 160, increasing quality does not restore the original 360. High/medium shadow sizes are the same; default v1 ignores most quality handling. Current query-string low/medium/high is not a complete or safe automatic quality system.
5. **Frame rate influences current simulation pacing:** `MarsWorld.tsx:506` clamps render-deriveddt to 0.05 and adds elapsed outside the active-state test; drive/energy/sol progression use that clock. Faster rendering can change the real time at which existing gameplay progresses. Performance work must not silently repair/redefine mission formulas or the 30-sol mechanics. Separately authorized deterministic simulation work and recorded-input invariance tests are required before claiming quality changes cannot affect outcomes. Cosmetic camera settling can use its own wall-clock interval without changing the simulation interval.
6. **Unbounded animation easing hurts interaction:** RIMFAX normal-motion click 18.476 s versus 3.571 s reduced-motion in the diagnostic environment; the node was visible and hittable. LOW quality must preserve marker size, focus, input and prompt clarity, not remove hotspots or crew interactions.
7. **First-frame/long-task stalls:** sequential rig preparation, texture decode/upload, compilation and terrain/PMREM generation all compete with user input. Download-complete is not model-ready. Existing placeholder/status text helps, but it does not explain the phase or allow retry everywhere.
8. **No real-device certification:** current 55 FPS visual-doc aspiration (`VISUALS.md:192`) is below the user's provisional 60 FPS mid-range target. Task 003's30/60 targets take priority for this plan. The audit cannot approve low-end playability from SwiftShader or 4× CPU results.

## 7. Proposed LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / AUTO design

This is a graphics-only design proposal. Implement after the gameplay invariance safeguards below are demonstrated. Use a dedicated visual-preference store, not fields that influence mission engineering. Persist manual preference outside campaign reset; show AUTO's current level and allow manual override at any time. A small settings overlay belongs inside the immersive world, not a new dashboard.

| Setting | LOW | MEDIUM | HIGH |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Target | 30 FPS active play on approved weak PC | 60 FPS on approved mid-range PC | 60 FPS with extra visual detail where measured headroom exists |
| Proposed effective render scale | 0.75–1.0× CSS resolution, absolute pixel cap tuned on device | 1.0–1.5× | 1.0–2.0×, still capped by measured budget |
| Shadows | One coherent key-light shadow, 512–1024 near-field; contact cue retained | 1024–1536 | 2048 near-field, bounded caster set |
| Foreground models | Identity-preserving near LOD, instruments/pivots always present | Same key geometry; higher distant LOD where visible | Best reviewed LOD |
| Textures | Reviewed 512–1024 maps, retain higher resolution only for perceptually critical faces/marks | Reviewed 1024–2048 | Up to reviewed originals where beneficial; no automatic upscaling |
| Environment | Near collision/science scenery fully represented; reduce far decorative detail | Balanced far detail | Additional distant detail without changing colliders |
| Effects | Reduced cosmetic dust/tracks, no expensive low-value effects | Balanced pools | Higher cosmetic pool budget; no additional gameplay events |
| Material/light policy | Preserve foreground PBR; simpler distant materials and fewer nonessential lights | Balanced PBR | Reviewed enhancements; no outcome changes |

These are starting ranges requiring measured tuning, not validated presets. Keep UI text at native CSS resolution. Maintain coherent warm key/sky fill, readable silhouettes, contact grounding, fog depth and recognizable faces/rover. Use 1:1.618 composition as a framing guide for focal subject/negative space and compact overlays where useful; do not change gameplay coordinates or claim the current layouts were measured to that ratio. Screen-space LOD should preserve the focal subject, including inspection closeups.

**AUTO algorithm proposal:**

1. Establish WebGL2 availability/context health, maximum texture size and supported compressed formats. Treat `deviceMemory`/`hardwareConcurrency` as coarse optional hints, not a GPU rating; do not rely on model-name whitelists or a single hardware hint. Unknown/limited/software device startsLOW; verified stronger device may startMEDIUM. Never startHIGH solely from a core count.
2. Warm up the scene and exclude loading/compilation, hidden tabs, resize and paused/modal interaction from classification. Measure real frame intervals/render timing, not the current debug overlay's `1/clamped dt` (which misleadingly floors reported FPS at 20 when real FPS is lower).
3. Evaluate robust rolling windows, for example 5 seconds, against the chosen 30/60 FPS budget. After several windows over budget, reduce render scale in small bounded steps, then shadow/decorative work. Preserve all hitboxes/controls/scene objects needed to understand collisions. Do not use v1↔v2 switching.
4. Use hysteresis and a cooldown (proposed 15 seconds); upgrade only after sustained spare capacity (proposed 30 seconds), one step at a time. Pause upgrades during model loading or interaction; never alternate profiles every frame. Manual mode stops automatic level changes but still handles context loss transparently.
5. Apply safe scale/effect changes between frames, not by remounting the whole mission scene. Recreate render targets only when size actually changes; cache baseline instance counts so LOW→HIGH restores them. Defer changes requiring a new context/large assets until an appropriate loading boundary with feedback.
6. Keep the exact same mission state, formulas, seed, scenario/event calendar, collision data, inputs and scientific results. Visual preferences are explicitly excluded from simulation/save-result hashes. A quality change must not reset a scan, position, battery, sol, order, accepted risk or outcome.

The numeric window/cooldown ranges are proposed tuning, not hidden gameplay timers. IfLOW still misses 30 FPS, retain controls and offer clear status/settings; never silently simplify the mission, change outcomes or remove the world.

## 9. Before/after measurement and regression gates

**Reproducibility:** record commit, lockfile/tool versions, asset hashes/bytes, browser/driver/OS, CPU/RAM/GPU, display resolution/DPR, power mode and thermal state. Use production builds served locally or the same controlled host; keep network conditions explicit. Run one browser workload at a time. Use fresh contexts/cache-disabled cold tests separately from byte-cache/HTTP-cache/prepared-template warm tests. Keep loading screenshots and timestamps for first usable world, first foreground model and all assets ready; don't substitute an early draw/status attribute.

**Performance protocol:** at least 3 runs per condition after warmup,≥60 seconds active play per scene and a sufficiently long capture for meaningful 1% tails (extend samples on very slow devices). Fixed route/input log, same camera, loadout, site and seed. Report averageFPS, slowest 1% mean-derivedFPS, P50/P95/P99/max frame ms, long tasks, loading stages, draw/triangle counts, JS heap, process RSS and GPU/driver memory where the platform exposes them. GPU timer queries are optional and must reject disjoint samples. Synchronous readback is for isolated completion probes only; do not insert it into the gameplay benchmark loop. Count renderer contexts/resources through 10 or more room→launch→surface→report→room cycles and rapid character switches; compare both natural-GC and controlled post-GC plateaus. Capture during changing quality, resize and context recovery.

**Provisional acceptance targets, requiring real-device confirmation:** user's30 FPS low-end and 60 FPS mid-range during active gameplay; propose 1% lows≥24/45 FPS respectively, minimal recurring>100 ms stalls after warmup, and no monotonic retained-memory growth across equivalent checkpoints. Proposed local loading budgets: first usable world≤2 seconds, warm selected-model ready≤1 second; slow network completion scales with actual bytes/bandwidth and must show progress rather than falsely time out. These numerical loading/tail budgets are proposed review targets, not achieved results or requirements added to GAMEPLAY.

**Gameplay invariants:** same ordered inputs and authoritative simulation steps must yield identical engineering checks, installed instruments, mass/energy/data ledgers, collision/hazard decisions, mission states, events, score and report acrossLOW/MEDIUM/HIGH/AUTO. Specifically exercise terrain boundaries, all rover instrument toggles, scan range/braking, wheel pivots/grounding, keyboard/touch hotspot access, crew navigation, repeated quality changes during actions, retry/damage states once implemented, save/load and hidden-tab handling. Physics colliders/hazard maps never follow visual instance-count/LOD reductions. Existing incompatible gameplay tests must be reconciled through Task 002's approved work, not weakened to make performance checks pass.

**Visual invariants:** fixed-view and animated-pose comparisons for every crew member/rover instrument at near/medium/far distances; face/hairstyle/uniform/silhouette recognition; stable shadows and grounding; legible science markers/hotspots and native text; terrain/rocks visibly match collision. Test light/dark views, 1440×1000, 1366×768 and 390/820px layouts, DPR 1/1.5/2, resize, loading fallback, context loss and manualAUTO overrides. Keep the world interactive; no dashboard replacement. Assess 1:1.618 framing where appropriate with the Director, without constraining necessary controls or masking hazards. Do not remove essential geometry/elements merely to meet a triangle budget.

**Reversible asset strategy:** keep originals and licenses untouched; derivative directory + manifest with source SHA256, exporter version/settings, triangle/material/texture inventory and intended LOD. Feature flag each derivative/codec; compare against original; revert by selecting original paths. Roll out one model then the remaining crew, then rover/environment. A failed conversion, decoder failure or incomplete fetch must preserve usable controls and recognizable fallback behavior. Any shared cache needs explicit ownership/ref-count tests before the current per-consumer disposal contract changes.
